**3D Printed RC Plane Build**
This project is a lightweight RC Plane to be printed using active foaming PLA. By using Aero PLA, the airframe stays light enough for slow speed handling while remaining rigid enough for basic aerobatics. 
The design is centered around a standard 2212 1000KV power system and an ELRS radio link for solid control range. Paired with a Radiomaster Pocket, you can easily get 20km of range.

Pros:
- Cheap & Simple
- Can make by your own using printer
- Easily adjustable and moddable
- Completely open source
- Easy to use guide
 

**Hardware and Electronics**

The build uses a 1000KV brushless motor paired with a 30A ESC and a 10-inch propeller. This setup provides plenty of thrust on a 3S 1500mAh LiPo battery. For the control link, the RadioMaster Pocket (ELRS version) is used in conjunction with a dedicated ELRS receiver. Stabilization is handled by an onboard flight controller, which is essential for managing the flight characteristics of a lightweight 3D-printed airframe.

**Printing and Material**

You need to use Bambu Lab PLA Aero for this build. Standard PLA is too dense and will result in a wing loading that is too high for the motor to handle effectively. When printing, use a single wall thickness and 0% infill to keep the weight at a minimum. Because Aero PLA expands at high temperatures, make sure to calibrate your flow rate—usually between 50% and 60%—to ensure the parts fit together without being overly heavy.

**Assembly and Flight Setup**

You can pretty much use any super glue from dollarama or wallmart. Avoid hot glue as it adds unnecessary weight and can melt the thin wall sections. Once the airframe is assembled, mount the motor and servos, ensuring all control surfaces move freely.

The most critical step is the Center of Gravity . Use the marks on the wing to balance the plane by shifting the 1500mAh battery forward or backward in the fuselage. Before the first flight, verify that your ELRS link is solid and that the flight controller orientation matches the physical mounting in the fuselage. A properly balanced plane should glide level when hand-launched at half throttle.
